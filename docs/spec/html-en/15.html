15 Core words

(1) A core word is written as a bare name, without use. The tables of this clause write signatures as in 9.2. T means “any one type”; it is a notation for built-in words only. The stack words (dup drop swap over nip) are in 9.3 and the combining words that take stems in 10.6.

15.1 Integers

(1) Arithmetic. Overflow and division by zero are faults (11.3).

WordSignatureMeaning

neg abs(int gives int)negation, absolute value; overflow for the smallest int
min max(int, int gives int)the smaller, the larger
clamp(n int, lo int, hi int gives int)n brought between lo and hi
rescale(d dec(N), M gives dec(M))the decimal with M digits after the point (M a constant); fewer digits rounded half away from zero (7.5)

(2) Words that treat overflow otherwise.

WordSignatureMeaning

chk_add chk_sub chk_mul chk_div(int, int gives int or fail)fail with err.overflow on overflow, err.div_zero on division by zero

(3) Words that read numbers as unsigned: the 64 bits as a number from 0 to 264−1.

WordSignatureMeaning
u_div u_mod(int, int gives int)unsigned quotient and remainder; division by zero is a fault
u_lt u_le u_gt u_ge(int, int gives bool)unsigned comparison
mul_hi u_mul_hi(int, int gives int)the upper 64 bits of the 128-bit product (signed, unsigned)

(4) Bits. Shift and rotate counts are taken modulo 64 (their low 6 bits). shl does not check for overflow.

WordSignatureMeaning
bit_and bit_or bit_xor(int, int gives int)bit by bit
bit_not(int gives int)every bit flipped
shl shr u_shr(n int, k int gives int)left, signed right, right filling with zeros
rotl rotr(n int, k int gives int)rotate
count_ones leading_zeros trailing_zeros(int gives int)the number of 1 bits, of leading and trailing zeros (64 for 0)
byte_swap(int gives int)the eight bytes in reverse order

(5) On a narrow integer (7.6) the words above take two of one type and give that type; overflow, wrapping and saturation are judged by that type’s range. The words that read numbers as unsigned and floor_div floor_mod are the plain words on an unsigned type and do not take a signed one. The bit words work within the width. neg abs min max clamp do not take a narrow integer; rotl rotr mul_hi u_mul_hi take u64 only.

(2) Examples are in clause 8.

(3) c sort puts the cells of an array, or of a list up to its length, in order from the smallest; c sort_desc from the largest. The cells change in place and the word gives nothing. The cells shall be int, a narrow integer, dec(N), bool (false first) or str(N), or of a type declared over one of them; u64 cells are compared unsigned, str(N) cells in the byte order of str.compare (16.1). A collection parameter (9.9) is sorted the same way. A list or array of records is refused: an index, a field, a global or a named row may keep its rows. Its rows are put in order through a list of their numbers, by (4).

15-sort.pnx

use io
list marks int 6
list names str(8) 4
rem the cells on one line, a blank between two
word show (cells list int)
  0 cells len range do (i int) i 0 gt if " " io.print end  cat (i cells idx) io.print end each
  "" io.println
end
word main (or fail)
  7 marks push or_fail  -2 marks push or_fail  12 marks push or_fail  0 marks push or_fail
  marks sort
  marks show
  marks sort_desc
  marks show
  "pear" names push or_fail  "Fig" names push or_fail  "apple" names push or_fail
  names sort
  0 names len range do (i int) i 0 gt if " " io.print end  i names idx io.print end each
  "" io.println
end
main or_else (e) e err_text io.println end

(4) o t sort_by r.f takes o, an array or list of int, each cell the number of a row of t; t, an array or list of records; and the field r.f of the record t holds, written after the word as to and set take their name. It puts the cells of o in order of that field of the rows they number, from the smallest; sort_by_desc from the largest. The field shall be of a type the cells of (3) may have. The rows do not move, so indexes and kept rows stay right. A cell that is not a row number of t is the fault range, found before any cell moves: o is left as it was. At most one of o and t may be a parameter.

15-sort-by.pnx

use io
record entry
  term str(8)
  count int
end
list terms entry 8
list order int 8
word count_in (t str, n int or fail)
  terms add_row or_fail to r
  t r to entry.term
  n r to entry.count
end
word main (or fail)
  "to" 4 count_in or_fail  "be" 2 count_in or_fail  "or" 2 count_in or_fail  "not" 1 count_in or_fail  "a" 4 count_in or_fail
  0 terms len range do (i int) i order push or_fail end each
  order terms sort_by entry.term
  order terms sort_by_desc entry.count       rem by count; the same count stays in byte order
  0 order len range do (i int)
    i order idx terms idx to r
    cat (r entry.count, " ", r entry.term) io.println
  end each
end
main or_else (e) e err_text io.println end

Output

4 a
4 to
2 be
2 or
1 not

(5) Both sorts are stable: cells of equal keys keep their order. A sort by several keys is several sorts, the least important key first.

(6) A sort does not fail. A load that sorts gives the program a work area of 8 bytes for each cell of the largest collection it sorts, or, when it sorts a collection parameter, of the largest array or list; the work area counts as a global of the state, and the block must have room for it. A sort of 𝑛 cells is charged to the budget as 𝑛·⌈log2𝑛⌉ instructions (19.5).
